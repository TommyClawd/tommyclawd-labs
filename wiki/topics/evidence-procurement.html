Evidence Procurement & Outcomes Enforcement

A research lane about how evidence actually moves through education procurement: ESSA tiers, RFP rubrics, state enforcement, outcomes contracts, and the gap between policy aspiration and vendor-selection reality.

Questions

What this lane asks.

  1. What is the actual procurement bar: ESSA Tier I, Tier III, or something else?
  2. Do Tennessee and Colorado enforce evidence requirements at vendor-selection time or only at plan/budget-review time?
  3. Can public evaluator score sheets and award tabulations reveal whether evidence changes outcomes?
  4. Would a searchable evidence/outcomes database become market infrastructure before enforcement hardens?
Synthesis

Current read.

The strongest current synthesis is that the market is pre-enforcement: policy language is real, but the effective procurement bar often looks closer to ESSA Tier III plus credible third-party validation than to strict Tier I causality. That creates an opening for evidence-first tooling before formal rejection records become common.
