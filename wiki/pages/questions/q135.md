### Q135 — Bounty Completion Rates and Solver Profitability: Do Solvers Systematically Overspend?

#### The Short Answer: Yes, for Most Solvers

The empirical data paints a consistent picture across HackerOne, Bugcrowd/Immunefi, and Gitcoin: bounty markets exhibit **extreme power-law earnings distributions** where a tiny elite profits handsomely while the median solver almost certainly overspends relative to their opportunity cost.

---

#### 1. HackerOne (Security Bug Bounties)

**Platform-level payouts (2024-2025):**
- $81M paid out in the 12 months ending mid-2025 (+13% YoY)
- Top 10 programs alone: $21.6M (26.7% of total)
- Top 100 all-time earners: $31.8M cumulative, with individuals "consistently surpassing six-figure annual earnings"
- Average yearly payout *across all active programs*: ~$42,000

**The distribution problem:**
- Top hunters earn $100K-$500K+/year; one (DeVoss) reported $903K in a year working 10-40 hours/month
- Santiago Lopez became the first to cross $1M lifetime on HackerOne
- But the *median* hunter earns far less. The $42K average is dragged up by whales; most hunters report long "bugless nights," duplicate submissions (no payout for second-finders), and programs where 100+ hours yield nothing
- NahamSec's framework: targets $500/medium-severity bug, expects 4-6 hours per bug *after* learning the app. But many hunters spend weeks learning an app before finding anything — effective hourly rate during ramp-up approaches zero
- The "100-Hour Rule" (community best practice): cap at 100 hours per program, then move on — explicitly acknowledging that unbounded time investment is the default failure mode

**Key structural feature:** Bounties pay for results, not time. Platform takes ~20% commission. This means the *buyer* gets enormous value (paying only for confirmed vulns), while solvers bear all exploration risk. This is the classic contest/tournament structure that economic theory predicts will attract too many entrants who collectively overspend.

#### 2. Smart Contract / Web3 Bounties (Immunefi, DeFi platforms)

- Immunefi: $100.21M total bounties paid out as of 2025; 77.5% to smart contract bugs
- Critical bugs: $10K-$1M range
- Automated research disclosed 10 high-value bugs totaling $3M+ in rewards
- Smart contract vulnerabilities caused ~$263M in damages in H1 2025 alone
- Global bug bounty platform market: $1.76B (2025) → projected $5.74B by 2034 (15.94% CAGR)

**Profitability signal:** The enormous damage-prevented figures ($263M in 6 months) vs. bounty payouts ($100M cumulative *ever*) suggests **buyers are massively underpaying** relative to value received. Solvers capture a tiny fraction of the value they create.

#### 3. Gitcoin (Open Source Development Bounties)

**The academic dataset** (arXiv:2309.15017 — study of Gitcoin Mainnet issues):
- 4,584 issues on Mainnet network
- **58.1% success rate** (2,662 issues completed); 41.9% non-success
- 81.7% of issues eventually closed; 18.3% remained open
- Largest bounty type: Feature development (40.4%), followed by Improvement
- Bounty values rarely changed after posting — price discovery is weak

**The 42% failure rate is the killer stat:** Nearly half of all posted bounties go uncompleted. This means either (a) the bounty was mispriced (too low to attract competent solvers), (b) the task was underspecified, or (c) solvers started and abandoned after realizing the effort exceeded the reward. All three scenarios represent systematic inefficiency.

#### 4. Google VRP (Comparison Benchmark)

- $17M paid to 747 researchers in 2025 (40% YoY increase, all-time high)
- $81.6M total since 2010
- Highest single reward: $250K
- Average per researcher: ~$22,750/year — barely a living wage in any developed country
- This is *Google*, the most prestigious program. The long tail is much worse.

#### The Systematic Overspend Mechanism

Connecting to Q042 (Winner's Curse in AI Bounties), the overspend operates at three levels:

1. **Solver-side tournament curse:** Too many entrants compete for fixed prizes. Each solver's expected value is negative when accounting for time, but they enter anyway due to (a) skill overconfidence, (b) learning value of the attempt, (c) option value of reputation/invitation to private programs, and (d) intrinsic motivation

2. **Duplicate/first-mover problem:** In security bounties, only the first valid reporter gets paid. Second-finders get nothing. This creates a pure waste of effort for all but one solver per vulnerability — a direct deadweight loss

3. **Information asymmetry:** Solvers can't observe how many others are working on the same target, can't estimate the difficulty distribution accurately, and face adverse selection (easy bugs are picked off quickly, leaving increasingly difficult ones)

**The learning-value offset:** Solvers partially compensate by treating bounties as paid education. This is rational for career-building but means the *cash economics* are even worse than they appear — solvers are implicitly subsidizing their own training.

#### Implications for M1 ($10M/5yr Outcomes Gap) and M3 (Information Markets)

- Bounty platforms are a **natural laboratory** for studying sorting efficiency in information markets
- The 42% Gitcoin failure rate and extreme HackerOne earnings skew are direct evidence of market design problems
- If education outcomes bounties follow the same pattern, expect: underpayment relative to value created, extreme solver concentration, high abandonment, and systematic overspend by the median participant
- **Design implication:** Any bounty-based outcomes market needs mechanisms to address the overspend problem — milestone payments, solver capacity signals, or cooperative (rather than competitive) structures
