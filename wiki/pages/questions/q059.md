### Q059: What Board-Level Pain Point Has No Good Software Solution?

#### The Core Finding: Board Decision-Quality Infrastructure

The research converges on a single massive gap: **boards have no software that helps them actually *think better* together under uncertainty.** The existing market (Diligent, OnBoard, BoardEffect) solves *logistics* — document distribution, meeting scheduling, e-signatures, compliance tracking. What nobody solves is the **cognitive layer**: helping 8-12 part-time directors synthesize conflicting signals across AI risk, geopolitics, climate, regulatory flux, and cyber into coherent strategic judgment.

#### The Evidence Stack

**1. The 90% Gap (BusinessWire, Jan 2026)**
"84% of directors have significantly changed their approach to scenario planning, but 90% lack AI tools to keep pace." Boards are *trying* to do strategic foresight and failing because no tool exists for it. Board portal vendors bolted on AI for document summarization — nobody built AI for *strategic reasoning under uncertainty*.

**2. The Structural Governance Gap (ECGI)**
"What boards struggle with is not how AI works, but how to ask the right questions, challenge management assumptions, and integrate [AI into oversight]." The gap isn't technical literacy — it's decision scaffolding. No software helps a director formulate the *right question* about an AI deployment they're overseeing.

**3. Static vs. Dynamic Governance (Corporate Governance Institute, 2026)**
Boards exhibit "static governance" — skillsets and processes that don't keep pace. The report calls for a shift to "dynamic" oversight but offers no tooling pathway. The entire solution set is "training + succession planning" because no product exists to operationalize dynamic governance.

**4. Information Overload + Decision Quality (Prairie Oyster analysis)**
The Substack piece identifies "Information Overload, Decision Quality, and Board Psychology" as a distinct risk category. Directors privately acknowledge feeling underprepared to interrogate AI risk. "Director self-censorship, where fear of 'asking the wrong question' keeps essential issues off the table." This is a UX problem masquerading as a competence problem.

**5. The Confidence-Competence Paradox (PwC 2025 Directors Survey)**
55% of directors say a colleague should be replaced. 61% of consumer-markets directors agree. Yet only 1/3 are "strongly confident" their board has the right collective skillset. The *assessment* of board capability is itself unsolved — boards can't even diagnose their own gaps systematically.

#### The Product Thesis

The unsolved pain point is **board-level decision intelligence**: software that helps directors (a) synthesize management inputs with external signals, (b) stress-test strategic assumptions via scenario modeling, (c) surface the right questions to ask, and (d) capture decision rationale beyond minutes.

This maps directly to **[M1] Outcomes Gap** — the product IS the evidence layer. For boards, the "outcome" is decision quality, and nobody measures or supports it with tooling. It also connects to **[M3] Information Markets** — board deliberation is a sorting/information-aggregation problem among a small group of high-stakes principals.

#### Why This Gap Persists

1. **Tiny user base per deployment** (8-12 directors) makes traditional SaaS unit economics hard
2. **Extreme trust requirements** — boards won't put strategic deliberation in a startup's cloud
3. **Consultants own the relationship** — McKinsey, Spencer Stuart, etc. solve this with billable hours, not software
4. **Directors are part-time** — engagement time is ~250 hours/year; tools must be zero-friction
5. **Incumbent portals have distribution** but no incentive to cannibalize their simple, profitable product

#### Market Size Signal

Diligent (acquired by Insight Partners for $4.6B in 2021) dominates board portals. The total board management software market is ~$5B. But the *decision intelligence* layer — if it existed — would command premium pricing from the same customers who already pay $50-150K/year for document distribution. The adjacent market for board advisory services (Spencer Stuart, Russell Reynolds, Heidrick) is another ~$3-5B that's ripe for partial software displacement.
