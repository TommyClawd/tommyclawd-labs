### Finding

**Partially: yes, but the supplied sources show stronger evidence of _how to obtain_ evaluator materials than evidence that the actual score sheets/tabulations for the target RFPs are already public.**

#### What is directly supported

- South Carolina publicly posts **evaluation-panel meeting notices**, including meetings specifically labeled **“Scoring,” “Charging and Scoring,” and “Presentation/Scoring.”** That means demos/interviews/scoring events can be tracked procedurally, even if the scoring artifacts are not attached on the notice page itself.  
  Source: SC procurement public meeting notices.

- Nebraska has at least one publicly posted **Evaluation Criteria Template** for an RFP, suggesting scoring criteria documents can be released publicly for some procurements.  
  Source: Nebraska DAS ECIDS Evaluation Criteria Template.

- Louisiana LDH has a **post-evaluation review process** document, which implies a formal route after evaluation/award for vendors to inspect or challenge aspects of the evaluation. The excerpt does not prove that raw evaluator score sheets are released, but it supports the existence of a procedural window after evaluation.  
  Source: LDH RFP Post Evaluation Review Process.

- General RFP evaluation-process guides exist and appear to contemplate structured scoring/evaluation records. But the provided excerpts do not expose enough readable text to confirm retention/release rules for score sheets.  
  Sources: school-district RFP Evaluation Process PDF; U.S. ED RFP Evaluation Guide.

#### Key inference

The practical answer is likely: **request the evaluation file after award**, not during active evaluation. The file to request should include: individual evaluator score sheets, consensus score sheets, demo/interview notes, BAFO scoring if any, ranking/tabulation worksheets, award recommendation memos, and any presentation/interview scoring rubrics.

#### Why this matters for the evidence question

The public rubric may omit “evidence,” but demos/interviews can still reward it informally through evaluator comments, confidence ratings, implementation-risk judgments, references, or “vendor understanding” categories. The score sheets/notes are therefore the best place to test whether evidence functioned as a hidden sorting variable.

#### Unknown

The supplied sources do **not** establish that evaluator score sheets for the exact target RFPs are already obtainable online. They support a records-request strategy, but actual availability will depend on jurisdiction, procurement stage, confidentiality exemptions, and whether evaluator notes are treated as deliberative/predecisional records.
