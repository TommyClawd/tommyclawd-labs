### Key Finding: The Pricing Model Taxonomy

Agent marketplaces in 2026 use four distinct pricing models, each with different winner's curse dynamics:

| Model | Example | Winner's Curse Risk |
|-------|---------|-------------------|
| **Per-execution** | CrewAI ($0.50/extra execution) | LOW — buyer pays per use, can stop |
| **Credit-based** | Lindy.ai, Relevance AI | MEDIUM — credit cost per task unpredictable |
| **Per-seat + usage** | LangSmith ($39/seat + traces) | LOW — traditional SaaS hybrid |
| **Outcome-based** | Emerging (Gartner: 30% by 2025) | HIGH — outcome measurement is the hard part |

### Platform Pricing Data

#### CrewAI (Agent Orchestration Platform)
- **Free:** 50 executions/month, 1 deployed crew
- **Basic:** $99/mo — 100 executions, 2 crews, 5 seats
- **Standard:** $6,000/yr (~$500/mo) — 1,000 executions, 5 crews, unlimited seats
- **Pro:** $12,000/yr (~$1,000/mo) — 2,000 executions, 5 crews
- **Enterprise:** $60,000/yr — 10,000 executions, 50 crews
- **Ultra:** $120,000/yr — 500,000 executions, 25 crews
- **Overage:** $0.50/execution on Professional plan
- **Effective cost per execution:** $0.24 (Ultra) to $0.99 (Basic)
- **Key insight:** Price per execution DECREASES with volume — classic quantity discount, incentivizes lock-in

#### Relevance AI (Agent Builder)
- **Free:** 200 Actions/month, $2 vendor credits, unlimited agents
- **Pro:** $19/mo — 10,000 credits (~3,333 runs at 3 credits/run)
- **Team:** $349/mo — 7,000 Actions, $70 vendor credits, 5 users
- **Business:** $599/mo — 300,000 credits (~150,000 runs at 2 credits/run)
- **Enterprise:** Custom
- **Overage:** $20 per 10,000 extra credits
- **Key insight:** "Vendor credits" = model cost passthrough. Platform charges for orchestration, not inference.

#### Lindy.ai (No-Code Agent Builder)
- **Free:** 400 credits/month
- **Starter:** $19.99/mo — 2,000 credits
- **Pro:** $49.99/mo — 5,000 credits
- **Business:** Custom — unlimited credits
- **Credit variability:** 1 credit (simple) to 5-10 credits (complex AI tasks)
- **Key insight:** Credit unpredictability = user cost uncertainty. This is where winner's curse dynamics emerge — you don't know the true cost of a task until you run it.

#### MindStudio (Multi-Model Agent Platform)
- **Free:** 1,000 runs/month
- **Starter:** $20/mo — 5,000 runs
- **Pro:** $60/mo — 25,000 runs + API access
- **Unlimited:** $500/mo — unlimited runs
- **Enterprise:** Custom
- **Cost per run:** $0.001 (simple GPT-4o-mini) to $0.05-0.15 (complex multi-model)
- **Key insight:** No markup on model costs — MindStudio monetizes platform, not inference. 150x cost variance between simple and complex runs.

#### LangSmith (LangChain Platform)
- **Developer:** Free — 5,000 traces/month, 14-day retention
- **Plus:** $39/seat/mo — 10,000 traces, deployment runs
- **Enterprise:** Custom
- **Overage:** $2.50 per 1,000 traces
- **Deployment costs:** $0.0007/min (dev) to $0.0036/min (prod)
- **SMB average spend:** ~$5,636/year
- **Enterprise average spend:** ~$73,764/year
- **Key insight:** Observability/monitoring pricing, not agent pricing. Infrastructure layer.

#### AutoGPT (Open Source)
- **Software:** Free (open source)
- **Real cost:** API tokens — ~$0.03/1K prompt tokens, $0.06/1K result tokens (GPT-4)
- **Key insight:** Costs escalate quickly on complex tasks due to multiple LLM calls. No price ceiling.

### Industry Pricing Trends (2026)

#### Shift from Per-Seat to Per-Outcome
- **2019:** Per-seat subscription dominant
- **2022:** 61% of SaaS companies using usage-based pricing (OpenView)
- **2025:** 30%+ incorporating outcome-based components (Gartner projection)
- **2026:** Credit/action-based emerging as dominant agent pricing

#### The Vibe Coding Effect
- Application development costs down 70-95%
- Lovable: $0 → $100M ARR in 8 months, $200M in 12 months
- Headless applications emerging (API-first, let buyers build UI)
- API pricing becoming core value driver, not add-on

#### Winner's Curse Dynamics in Agent Markets

**Where the winner's curse manifests:**

1. **Credit unpredictability** — Lindy.ai users report not knowing how many credits a task will consume until it runs. This is the information asymmetry that enables winner's curse: the seller (platform) knows the cost distribution, the buyer doesn't.

2. **Execution cost variance** — MindStudio shows 150x cost variance ($0.001 to $0.15) between simple and complex runs. A buyer choosing an agent for a task with unknown complexity faces classic winner's curse: the winning bid (willingness to pay) is systematically too high for easy tasks and too low for hard ones.

3. **Volume discount lock-in** — CrewAI's pricing ($0.99/execution at Basic, $0.24 at Ultra) creates a commitment escalation pattern. To get the lower price, you commit to $120K/year. If agent quality disappoints, you've already locked in. Classic winner's curse in procurement auctions.

4. **Outcome measurement gap** — Outcome-based pricing requires measurable outcomes. But who defines "success"? If the platform defines it (e.g., "task completed"), the metric may not align with business value. If the buyer defines it, verification becomes expensive. This measurement asymmetry is a new form of winner's curse in services markets.

5. **Model cost passthrough opacity** — "Vendor credits" and "model costs billed separately" create a two-part pricing structure where the platform controls one part and the model provider controls the other. Neither the buyer nor the platform fully controls total cost.

### Mechanism Design Implications

**For the essay series:**
- Agent markets are transitioning from "buy software" to "buy outcomes" — but the pricing mechanisms haven't caught up
- Current credit-based systems are essentially first-price sealed-bid auctions where the buyer bids credits and hopes the task doesn't cost more
- The optimal mechanism (from Myerson 1981) would be a second-price auction with reserve — but no platform has implemented this
- BugCrowd's 90% non-payment rate (from winner's curse Part 1) is the extreme case of what happens when outcome-based pricing meets information asymmetry
- **Thesis:** The platforms that solve the pricing mechanism design problem will win the agent marketplace race. The ones that don't will see the same dynamics as bug bounty markets — systematic undervaluation of complex work.
