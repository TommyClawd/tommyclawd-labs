### Q177: Power-Law Scaling Exponents in Brand Perception During Identity Transitions

#### Short Answer
**No direct evidence found** of published work fitting power-law scaling exponents specifically to brand perception time-series during identity transitions. The sources provided were entirely irrelevant (corrupted PDFs, a TfL contracts register, corporate annual reports, and an IT services blog). However, the question sits at a well-studied intersection, and adjacent literatures provide partial answers.

#### What Exists (inferred from domain knowledge)

**1. Brand tracking firms collect the data but don't publish power-law fits.**
YouGov (one source was their annual report), Kantar, Ipsos, and Morning Consult all run continuous brand trackers (BrandIndex, BrandZ, Brand Health Tracking). These produce dense time-series of NPS, consideration, awareness, and buzz scores — exactly the data you'd need. But their published analyses use simple trend lines, moving averages, or event-study windows. Power-law modeling isn't part of their commercial reporting vocabulary.

**2. The rebranding/identity transition literature is case-study heavy, not quantitative-dynamics heavy.**
Academic marketing journals (JMR, JCR, Marketing Science) cover identity transitions primarily through:
- Pre/post surveys (Muzellec & Lambkin 2006 on corporate rebranding)
- Event studies on stock price (Lee 2014 on name changes)
- Qualitative brand architecture frameworks

None of these fit continuous decay/recovery curves, let alone test for power-law vs. exponential vs. logistic dynamics.

**3. Power-law dynamics in opinion/sentiment time-series exist — but in different literatures.**
- **Computational social science:** Power-law relaxation in collective attention (Wu & Huberman 2007 on Digg; Crane & Sornette 2008 on YouTube views). These show attention decays as t^(-α) after spikes.
- **Crisis communication:** Pang et al. and others model sentiment recovery after PR crises — some fit exponential decay, but power-law tests are rare.
- **Political opinion dynamics:** Voter intention time-series have been tested for long-range correlations and Hurst exponents (power-law memory), but not mapped to brand identity.

**4. The gap is specific and publishable.**
The missing piece: taking a continuous brand tracker (e.g., YouGov BrandIndex daily data for a company that underwent a major rebrand — Meta/Facebook, Twitter/X, Google/Alphabet) and fitting power-law exponents to the perception recovery curve. The data exists commercially. The statistical tools exist (MLE power-law fitting à la Clauset, Shalizi & Newman 2009). Nobody has connected them.

#### Why the Gap Exists
- Brand tracking data is proprietary and expensive
- Marketing academics favor experimental/survey designs over time-series econometrics
- Physicists who study power laws don't study brands
- The interdisciplinary bridge (computational marketing science) is small

#### Connection to Focus Areas
- **[M2] Identity as Compression:** A power-law decay in brand perception = slow decompression of the old identity encoding. The exponent would literally measure how tightly the old brand was compressed into consumer memory.
- **[M1] Outcomes Gap:** If you could show brand transitions follow predictable scaling laws, that's a consulting product — predict recovery timelines from early data points.
