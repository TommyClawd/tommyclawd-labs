### R4A's 2025 'Invest in What Works' State Standard of Excellence — Procurement & Evidence-Based Mandates

#### What the Index Measures
The 2025 edition (the latest) uses **12 criteria / 35 subcriteria** organized into three thematic areas: **Building Evidence**, **Using Learning**, and **Delivering Results**. It assessed **45 U.S. states** and recognized/certified 14+13+1 = 28 states at various tiers.

#### The Procurement-Closest Criterion: #6 — "Investing in What Works through Grants and Contracts"
Criterion 6 is the direct proxy for evidence-based procurement mandates. Sub-criterion 6.1 asks:

> *Does the state define evidence of effectiveness and require or reward it in grants and contracts?*

States can score up to **30 points** via either:
- **Volume path:** 1 point per $15M spent through evidence-prioritizing grants, OR 1 pt per grant program that defines/prioritizes evidence (whichever is greater).
- **Mandate path (the 'procurement mandate' shortcut):** A **statewide policy** (law, executive order, admin rule) that requires *all competitive grant programs in economic mobility agencies* to define and prioritize evidence of effectiveness — automatically scores the full 30 points.

This is the clearest benchmark for which states are closest to evidence-based procurement *mandates* as distinct from ad hoc evidence use.

#### Leading State on Criterion 6: Tennessee
Tennessee is cited as the **leading example** for Criterion 6 in 2025. Tennessee has had an evidence-based grants framework embedded in statute and executive policy for several years.

#### 2025 Tier Structure (14 Recognized + 13 Certified + 1 HM)
| Tier | States |
|------|--------|
| **Platinum Certified** | Colorado, Minnesota, Tennessee |
| **Gold Certified** | Maryland, North Carolina, Rhode Island |
| **Silver Certified** | Connecticut, Michigan, New Jersey, New Mexico, Ohio, Pennsylvania, Utah |
| **Honorable Mention** | Oregon |

All 14 "recognized" states (Platinum through Silver) met criteria across multiple dimensions including Criterion 6. The Platinum trio — **Colorado, Minnesota, Tennessee** — are structurally closest to full evidence-based procurement mandates, having scored highly on both the grant/contract evidence criterion AND evaluation leadership.

#### Key Structural Insight for the $10M/5yr Thesis

The R4A index reveals a **two-track convergence** toward evidence-based procurement:

1. **Bottom-up accumulation:** States like Ohio and Pennsylvania score via sheer volume of evidence-tagged spending ($15M increments). This is adoption without mandate.
2. **Top-down mandate:** States like Tennessee and Colorado score via statewide policies requiring evidence in competitive grants. This is the structural mandate.

The gap between these tracks is the **product opportunity**: tools that help Track 1 states cross into Track 2 by making evidence-tagging cheap enough to mandate universally. The What Works Toolkit (R4A's implementation guide for state/local agencies) explicitly provides templates for this transition — moving from "compliance-focused" to "outcomes-focused" contracting.

#### Complementary: Workforce Evidence-Based Spending Guide
R4A also publishes a **Workforce Evidence-Based Spending Guide** specifically targeting state/local workforce development procurement, with 6 strategies for moving beyond compliance-based procurement. This is narrower but directly maps to WIOA/workforce contract cycles.

#### Limitations
- The index is self-reported + R4A-verified, not independently audited.
- "Evidence" definitions vary: some states count "promising" evidence, others require RCT-level rigor.
- The index doesn't capture *enforcement* — a mandate on paper may not change procurement behavior.
- No state has achieved a universal evidence-based procurement mandate across ALL spending (not just economic mobility agencies).
