### Q171: ESSA Evidence Tiers as Procurement Gates — State-Level Timeline

#### What ESSA Actually Requires (Federal Level)

ESSA (2015) established four evidence tiers but **did not make them hard procurement gates federally**. The tiers are:
- **Tier 1 (Strong):** RCT, meets WWC standards without reservations, ≥350 students, ≥2 sites, statistically significant positive effect
- **Tier 2 (Moderate):** Quasi-experimental, meets WWC standards with/without reservations
- **Tier 3 (Promising):** Correlational with statistical controls
- **Tier 4 (Demonstrates a Rationale):** Logic model based on research, with ongoing evaluation planned

Critically: ESSA requires evidence-based interventions for **specific federal funding streams** (Title I school improvement 1003(a), Title II, Title IV, direct student services). It does **not** mandate evidence tiers for general district procurement. The evidence requirement is a **funding eligibility condition**, not a universal procurement gate.

#### Current State of Play: Soft Preferences, Not Hard Gates

**No state has yet made ESSA tiers a hard procurement gate for general edtech/curriculum purchasing.** The landscape is:

1. **Federal funding-linked requirements** — When districts use Title I school improvement dollars (the bottom 5% of schools), they must use Tier 1-3 interventions. This is the closest thing to a "hard gate" and it's federal, not state.

2. **State-level evidence preferences (leading states):**
   - **Louisiana** — Arguably the furthest along. Their curriculum transparency/review system (Curriculum Reviews) rates materials and strongly steers districts toward reviewed/approved curricula. Not a formal ESSA-tier gate, but functions as a de facto procurement filter.
   - **Texas** — TEA's approved instructional materials list creates a soft gate; districts choosing off-list materials lose state funding for those purchases.
   - **Tennessee** — Early adopter of evidence-based practices requirements; state review processes for textbook adoption reference evidence quality.
   - **Massachusetts, Connecticut** — Have integrated evidence standards into competitive grant programs.

3. **Results for America's "Invest in What Works" index** tracks state adoption of evidence-based policymaking. States scoring highest tend to embed evidence preferences in grant programs first, general procurement second.

#### Timeline Assessment

**The transition from soft preference → hard gate is not on a clear legislative timeline anywhere.** The pattern observed:

- **2015-2020:** States built awareness and WWC/ESSA literacy
- **2020-2024:** States began embedding evidence preferences in competitive grants and school improvement plans
- **2024-2026:** A few states (LA, TX) have de facto gates through approved lists, but these are curriculum-review gates, not formally ESSA-tier gates
- **Projected 2026-2030:** The most likely pathway to hard gates is through **state-level procurement reform tied to budget pressure**, not through ESSA reauthorization. As district budgets tighten and accountability pressure increases, evidence requirements will harden — but piecemeal, state by state.

#### Why This Matters for M1 ($10M/5yr Outcomes Gap Product)

The gap between current reality (soft preferences) and the anticipated future (hard gates) is **the market window**. A product that helps vendors achieve Tier 1-2 evidence — or helps districts evaluate evidence quality — has value now as a competitive differentiator and will have increasing value as gates harden. The key insight: **the gate-hardening will be driven by state procurement offices and school boards, not federal legislation.** This means the sales motion is state-by-state, and early-mover states (LA, TX, TN) are the beachhead markets.

#### What's Unknown

- No public legislative tracker exists specifically for "ESSA tier as procurement requirement" bills at the state level
- The Trump/current administration's ED posture on evidence-based policy enforcement is uncertain and may slow federal pressure
- Whether ESSA itself gets reauthorized or replaced (it's overdue) could reset the entire framework
