### Q189: Evidence-Based Procurement Enforcement in "Platinum" States

The provided sources contain **zero direct evidence** about education-technology procurement enforcement in Tennessee or Colorado — no RFPs rejected for lacking evidence, no procurement officer interviews, no state audit reports on evidence-based mandates.

#### What we can infer from adjacent domains

1. **Procurement mandates are routinely aspirational.** The federal contractor vaccine mandate (2021) showed that even explicit federal procurement requirements faced immediate legal challenges and uneven enforcement across states. Mandates ≠ enforcement.

2. **"Enforcement" in procurement typically means gating, not rejection.** The Latenode community thread illustrates the pattern: centralized policy enforcement works when it's *built into the workflow* (the system blocks non-compliant actions). When enforcement depends on human reviewers applying discretionary criteria like "evidence-based," compliance degrades to post-hoc audit.

3. **Evidence-based mandates in adjacent fields (law enforcement training) remain aspirational.** The d'Vinci article (2026) frames evidence-based training as a best practice to aspire to, not a procurement gate. CPOA's evidence-based policing work similarly treats it as advocacy, not a hard procurement filter.

#### The likely reality (inferred, not sourced)

- **Tennessee and Colorado likely have evidence-based language in procurement policy or statute**, but enforcement almost certainly operates as a *scoring preference* (extra points for evidence) rather than a *hard gate* (automatic rejection without RCT data).
- Procurement officers are generalists — they verify compliance checkboxes (FERPA, accessibility, insurance), not research methodology. "Evidence-based" likely means the vendor *claims* evidence, not that the claim is independently validated.
- The real enforcement mechanism is probably **vendor self-attestation** reviewed by program staff, not procurement officers.

#### What would answer this definitively

- Actual TN/CO education procurement RFP templates showing evidence requirements
- Interviews with state procurement officers or edtech vendors who've been through the process
- State audit reports on procurement compliance
- ESSA Tier classification usage in state purchasing decisions
