### Winner's Curse in AI Bounties — Synthesis

The sources converge on three distinct manifestations of the winner's curse in AI/tech contexts:

#### 1. Model-Race Winner's Curse (Nadella/Platform Economics)
Satya Nadella's warning: winning the AI model race means absorbing massive training costs while the model commoditizes rapidly. The real moat is ecosystem (data pipelines, agent frameworks, trust layers, distribution), not the model itself. Historical parallels: BlackBerry→Apple, Nokia→Android, Netscape→Microsoft. **This is the classic overpayment-for-the-trophy pattern applied to corporate strategy.**

#### 2. Policy Evaluation Winner's Curse (Bastani et al., 2026)
The arXiv paper (2602.08892) provides the strongest academic anchor: in data-driven decision-making, model-based policy evaluation systematically produces optimistic estimates due to winner's curse. They surveyed 55 Management Science papers from the past decade — **all but two** used this flawed methodology. Common justifications (model accuracy, random assignment, sample splitting) fail to eliminate the bias. This directly maps to bounty markets: the winning submission is selected *because* it looks best on the evaluation metric, which inflates its apparent value.

#### 3. Acquisition Winner's Curse (Egan & Brander)
Classic corporate finance application — acquiring privately-held firms under information asymmetry. Aggravated by more bidders and greater asymmetry. Relevant to AI bounty markets because bounty platforms create exactly these conditions: many bidders, asymmetric information about solution quality, and selection on noisy signals.

#### The Bounty-Specific Synthesis
AI bounties exhibit a *compounded* winner's curse:
- **Solver side:** The winner overspends effort relative to expected payout (classic auction theory)
- **Funder side:** The winning solution is selected on evaluation metrics that overestimate real-world performance (Bastani's policy evaluation curse)
- **Platform side:** The platform that "wins" the bounty market overpays for growth while the model commoditizes (Nadella's ecosystem curse)

This three-layer structure is the novel contribution angle for the essay — most treatments only address one layer.
