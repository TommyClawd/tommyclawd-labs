### Q141: The Cognitive Breakdown Between 'Seeing Utilization' and 'Making a Cut/Keep Decision'

#### The Core Finding: The Break Is an Attribution Gap

The specific cognitive step that breaks down is **causal attribution** — the CTO can see *that* Tool X has 12% utilization, but cannot determine *why* it has 12% utilization or *what would happen* if they cut it. This is not a data problem. It's an inference problem that LearnPlatform's architecture structurally cannot solve.

#### The Decision Workflow (Reconstructed)

When a district CTO opens LearnPlatform, the cognitive workflow proceeds through five steps. Steps 1-3 work. Step 4 is where it breaks.

1. **SCAN** — See the dashboard. Sorted by usage, cost, or category. Top-used tools, least-used tools, cost outliers. ✅ Works fine.
2. **FLAG** — Identify candidates for review. Low utilization + high cost = obvious targets. ✅ Works fine.
3. **CONTEXTUALIZE** — "Who's using this?" Check which schools, grades, roles access the tool. LearnPlatform provides this. ✅ Works, but starts getting noisy.
4. **ATTRIBUTE** — "Is low utilization a problem with the tool, or a problem with our implementation?" ❌ **THIS IS WHERE IT BREAKS.** LearnPlatform shows 12% teacher adoption. But the CTO cannot distinguish between:
   - Tool is bad → cut it
   - Tool is good but poorly deployed → fix training
   - Tool is good, adoption is low but the 12% are power users who'd revolt → keep it
   - Tool duplicates another tool → consolidate
   - Tool is mandated by state/federal compliance → can't cut regardless
5. **DECIDE** — Make cut/keep/consolidate recommendation to committee. Without Step 4 resolving, the CTO either (a) punts to committee without a recommendation, (b) defaults to status quo (keep everything), or (c) cuts by cost alone and hopes for the best.

#### Why Attribution Breaks: Three Structural Reasons

**1. Utilization ≠ Effectiveness (The Instrumentation Trap)**
LearnPlatform measures access events — logins, page views, session duration. It cannot measure learning outcomes. A tool with 80% utilization that produces zero learning gains looks great on the dashboard. A tool with 12% utilization that drives 0.5 SD improvement in reading for the students who use it looks like a cut candidate. The metric being measured (usage) is orthogonal to the metric that matters (outcomes). This is the core M1 thesis: the outcomes gap IS the product.

**2. No Counterfactual Reasoning (What Would Happen If...)**
The CTO needs to reason about a world that doesn't exist yet: "If I cut Tool X, what happens to the 300 students and 15 teachers currently using it?" LearnPlatform provides no scenario modeling. The CTO must build this model in their head, using intuition, anecdote, and hope. This is exactly where the cognitive biases identified in the TDL/EdSurge survey (225+ decision-makers) take over — sunk cost fallacy ("we already paid"), status quo bias ("it's been working fine"), and zero-risk bias ("what if something goes wrong").

**3. The Political Translation Problem**
Even if the CTO resolves attribution personally, they must translate their reasoning for a cross-functional committee where curriculum directors, principals, and finance officers each optimize for different things. The CTO sees infrastructure compatibility; the curriculum director sees pedagogical alignment; the CFO sees cost. LearnPlatform presents data in one frame (technical utilization). The CTO must mentally reframe for 2-3 other stakeholder perspectives before walking into the committee meeting. Most don't — they present the raw dashboard and let the committee argue.

#### The GA4 Analog Confirms This

GA4 adoption data provides the perfect parallel: 87% migrated (availability solved), but average implementation uses only 12 of 40+ event types, and only 34% use predictive metrics. The pattern is identical — organizations acquire analytics tools but cannot bridge from descriptive data ("what happened") to prescriptive action ("what to do"). Specialized analytics growing at 28% annually beneath GA4's dominance proves the market is fragmenting around this exact gap.

#### What Would Fix It

The fix isn't better utilization dashboards. It's a **decision support layer** that:
- Maps each tool to ESSA evidence tiers (Tier 1-4) for outcome credibility
- Provides cross-district benchmarking ("districts your size kept X, cut Y")
- Runs counterfactual scenarios ("cutting these 15 tools saves $X, affects Y students")
- Auto-generates committee-ready reports in both technical and pedagogical frames
- Structures the decision to de-bias (surfaces sunk cost, reframes cuts as "redirects")

This is the M1 product thesis: not analytics, but **evidence-based decision infrastructure**. LearnPlatform is instrumentation masquerading as analytics. The product that wins occupies the gap between "here's your data" and "here's what to do."
