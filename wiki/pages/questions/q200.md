### Q200: What Do TN/CO Education Procurement RFPs Actually Require for Evidence?

#### The ESSA Evidence Tier System (Federal Baseline)

ESSA Section 8101(21)(A) defines four evidence tiers that ripple into all federal education spending:

| Tier | Label | Requirement | Study Design |
|------|-------|------------|-------------|
| I | Strong | Experimental study, statistically significant positive effect | RCT (randomized control trial) |
| II | Moderate | Quasi-experimental, statistically significant causal effect | Matched comparison groups with statistical corrections |
| III | Promising | Correlational with statistical controls for bias | Pre/post with controls, no comparison group required |
| IV | Demonstrates Rationale | Logic model based on research; ongoing evaluation planned | Theory + commitment to study |

#### What Districts Actually Buy At

**The critical finding:** Most district procurement decisions operate at **Tier III (Promising Evidence)**, not Tier I. LXD Research (a firm specializing in ESSA certification) states explicitly: *"For the majority of district procurement decisions, ESSA Tier III is not the consolation prize. It's what buyers are actually looking for."*

Tier III requires:
- Outcome measurement (before/after)
- Statistical controls for bias
- Statistically significant positive effect
- Minimum ~50 students in sample
- Does **NOT** require a comparison group or large effect size

A portfolio of multiple Tier III studies across contexts can be more persuasive than a single Tier I study in one context.

#### State-Level Procurement Mechanisms

**Tennessee:**
- TN created **preferred provider lists** under ESSA, requiring vendors to demonstrate evidence tier alignment
- State-level RFPs (e.g., the Pearson assessment contract) include "evidence-based" as explicit evaluation criteria
- The TN ESSA State Plan requires districts using federal improvement funds (Title I school improvement, CSI/TSI) to select interventions meeting at least Tier III evidence
- Districts have had "uncomfortable conversations" with long-time vendors who couldn't demonstrate ESSA tier evidence (Project Evident/National Comprehensive Center, 2021)

**Colorado:**
- CDE's federal programs monitoring requirements include procurement documentation with rationale
- Evidence requirements flow through federal funding compliance rather than state-specific RFP templates
- Colorado follows the national pattern: ESSA tier requirements are **mandatory for federally-funded interventions**, advisory/preferred for general fund purchases

#### The Actual RFP Landscape

**What RFPs typically require (synthesized from Project Evident's procurement analysis):**
1. Do providers utilize evidence-based practices? Which parts of their model are evidence-based?
2. If not evidence-based, what impact did their model demonstrate in similar communities?
3. What training do providers deliver and require?
4. What data/reporting commitments does the vendor make?

**Key distinction:** RFPs rarely say "cite your ESSA tier" as a checkbox. Instead they:
- Ask vendors to describe their evidence base narratively
- May reference ESSA tiers in scoring rubrics (higher tier = more points)
- Require evidence documentation when federal funds are involved (mandatory)
- Accept vendor-commissioned studies reviewed by third parties (Marzano Research, Digital Promise, EduEvidence)

#### Third-Party Validation Industry

A cottage industry has emerged around ESSA evidence certification:
- **Digital Promise** — Research-Practice Partnership framework, widely recognized by districts
- **EduEvidence** — Independent methodology review, publishes findings publicly
- **What Works Clearinghouse (WWC)** — Federal gold standard, IES-operated
- **Evidence for ESSA (Johns Hopkins)** — Searchable database of rated programs

Vendors like IXL invest heavily: Johns Hopkins RCT for Tier I, plus multiple peer-reviewed quasi-experimental studies for Tier II. Edmentum's Study Island got Marzano Research peer review for Tier II. These are competitive differentiators in procurement.

#### Implications for the $10M Outcomes Gap Product

1. **Tier III is the market-entry bar**, not Tier I. A well-designed pre/post study with 50+ students and statistical controls clears it.
2. **Vendor claims alone are insufficient** for federally-funded purchases. You need at least a study document, ideally third-party reviewed.
3. **The evidence requirement is binding only for federal dollars** (Title I, CSI/TSI, ESSER successor funds). General fund purchases may accept vendor claims, but competitive RFPs increasingly score evidence.
4. **Study design matters more than journal publication.** Districts care about the WWC/ESSA tier rating more than where it's published. A vendor white paper reviewed by Marzano Research or Digital Promise carries more weight in procurement than an obscure journal article.
5. **Publication path optimization:** Get a Tier III study → third-party review → list on Evidence for ESSA database. That's the distribution channel that matters, not academic journals.
