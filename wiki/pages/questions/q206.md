### Q206 answer: actual TN/CO district edtech rubrics usually do **not** score evidence as its own large category

Across the supplied Tennessee and Colorado district-level RFPs, the pattern is clear: **price and product/functionality dominate; implementation/support is often meaningful; evidence/research is usually a gate, a narrative requirement, or embedded inside technical fit rather than a high-point standalone category.**

#### Scoring pattern from these specific RFPs

If we normalize only the explicit numeric rubrics above:

- **Price:** usually **10–45%**, with observed examples at 10, 15, 20, 20, 25, 30, and 45 points/percent.
- **Implementation/support:** usually **embedded**, but when explicit it is around **10–20%**. It can be larger if counted inside “approach/methodology.”
- **Technical/functionality/product fit:** usually the biggest real scoring bucket, often **30–80%** depending on how categories are grouped.
- **Vendor qualifications / references / past performance:** ranges from **5–45%**; sometimes dominates when the district is buying services or assistive/specialized tools.
- **Evidence of educational effectiveness:** in these examples, **0% explicit standalone scoring**. The closest forms are:
  - mandatory research/evidence requirements, especially White County TN;
  - prequalification/evidence-aligned partner guide, as in Poudre CO;
  - references/past performance, which are evidence of delivery, not evidence of learning impact;
  - outcome/reporting capability requirements, which are not the same as causal impact evidence.

#### The deep thing

The market reality is sharper than “evidence gets 5–15%.” In these district examples, **evidence often gets zero competitive points because procurement has converted it into either compliance language or product-fit language.** That means an evidence-first edtech product does not automatically win traditional RFPs by having better study design. It wins if that evidence can be translated into one of three procurement-native advantages:

1. **Gate advantage:** competitors fail mandatory “research-based/proven” or ESSA-aligned requirements.
2. **Technical/functionality advantage:** evidence is operationalized as assessment validity, reporting, dosage, fidelity tracking, benchmark alignment, and measurable growth workflows.
3. **Implementation advantage:** the vendor proves it can drive adoption, training, monitoring, and support — because districts score “can this actually work here?” more readily than “is the research design beautiful?”

#### Cite vs infer vs unknown

**Cited from the supplied RFPs:** the point weights above for Memphis-Shelby, Knox County, Poudre, Colorado BOCES, White County, and MNPS; the qualitative Kingsport rubric; White County’s mandatory research/proven-solution language; Poudre’s evidence-aligned partner-guide requirement.

**Inferred:** evidence is economically important but procurement-hidden: it affects eligibility, credibility, and technical scoring, even when no category says “evidence = X points.”

**Unknown:** whether internal evaluator score sheets, demo scoring sheets, or post-shortlist interviews added hidden evidence weighting not visible in the public RFP PDFs. Public RFPs may understate what evaluators discuss in committee.
