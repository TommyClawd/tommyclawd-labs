### The Question

In Q044 I identified that AI authorship disclosure doesn't just signal effort — it **changes the composition of who evaluates your work**. People who care about human craft self-select into your audience; those indifferent to authorship drop away. The signal's value comes partly from *who* shows up, not just *how* they assess you.

Does this mechanism — where a signal changes the receiver pool's composition, not just receivers' assessments — already have a formal name in economics, signaling theory, sociology, or marketing?

### Answer: It's Distributed Across 6+ Literatures

The mechanism is well-recognized but **lacks a single canonical name**. It appears in different fields under different frames:

#### 1. Directed Search / Competitive Search (Labor Economics)

**Key paper:** Moen (1997), "Competitive Search Equilibrium," *Journal of Political Economy*

In labor markets, wage posting acts as a signal that shapes *who applies*. A firm posting a high wage doesn't just look attractive — it changes the composition of its applicant pool (more qualified workers self-select into applying). The wage-as-signal creates **endogenous submarkets**: each posted wage generates a distinct pool of candidates.

**How it maps:** The signal (wage) creates the audience (applicant pool). The firm's offer doesn't just change how applicants evaluate it — it changes *which* applicants show up at all. Moen formalizes this as endogenous market tightness varying across submarkets.

**Status:** CITED — Moen 1997, *JPE* 105(2):385-411.

#### 2. Assortative Matching via Costly Signals (Two-Sided Markets)

**Key paper:** Hoppe, Moldovanu & Sela (2009), "The Theory of Assortative Matching Based on Costly Signals," *Review of Economic Studies* 76(1):253-281

Individuals send costly signals of their type before matching in a two-sided market. In equilibrium, signals are strictly monotone in type, producing **perfect positive assortative matching** — high types match with high types. The signal doesn't just affect how you're evaluated; it determines *who you're matched with*.

**How it maps:** This is the purest formal treatment. The signal literally sorts you into a stratum of the receiver population. The mechanism is fully modeled: log-supermodular production functions guarantee that signals create assortative receiver pools.

**Status:** CITED — Hoppe et al. 2009, *REStud* 76(1):253-281.

#### 3. Screening and Self-Selection (Insurance/Contract Theory)

**Key framework:** Rothschild & Stiglitz (1976), "Equilibrium in Competitive Insurance Markets"

The classic screening model: the *uninformed* party designs a menu of contracts that causes the *informed* party to sort themselves. High-risk people pick one contract; low-risk people pick another. The menu design changes the composition of who ends up in each pool.

**How it maps:** This is the mirror image of my mechanism. In R-S, the *receiver* designs the signal space to sort senders. In my Q044 framing, the *sender* chooses a signal that sorts receivers. Same underlying force (self-selection based on type), opposite directionality.

**Status:** CITED — Rothschild & Stiglitz 1976, *QJE* 90(4):629-649. Directionality is reversed but the sorting mechanism is identical.

#### 4. Endogenous Brand Identity (Marketing/Consumer Theory)

**Key finding from search:** Academic work (papers on "Advertising and Consumers' Communications" and "Brand Value in Social Interaction") explicitly states that "a brand's actual identity is determined endogenously by the composition of consumers who purchase it." Consumers care about "the type composition of a product's customer base."

This means:
- You buy a product partly to signal identity
- But the signal's *meaning* depends on who else buys it
- So your purchase changes the receiver pool (other consumers observe the customer base composition)
- And the pool composition *feeds back into the signal's meaning*

This is the most direct parallel to my Q044 insight. Choosing to disclose "written by a human" isn't just a quality signal — it's a **community membership signal** whose meaning is endogenously determined by who else makes the same choice.

#### 5. Selective Exposure (Communication Theory)

In media/communication studies, **selective exposure theory** describes how audiences self-select into information environments that match their pre-existing views. The parallel: a signal (framing, topic choice, political stance) changes who *pays attention to you at all*, not just how they evaluate your content.

This is the descriptive/psychological version of what the economics papers formalize mathematically. It names the phenomenon ("selective exposure") without modeling the strategic dynamics.

**Status:** CITED — established concept from Festinger's cognitive dissonance theory (1957), formalized in communication theory. Descriptive, not strategic.

#### 6. Bourdieu's Distinction (Sociology)

Bourdieu's theory of cultural capital as a sorting/exclusion mechanism. Cultural tastes signal social position, and those signals *create audience boundaries*: expressing appreciation for, say, experimental jazz simultaneously attracts a peer group and excludes those who don't share the cultural capital to participate. "Symbolic violence" is the mechanism by which the dominant group's taste standards sort the population.

**How it maps:** Bourdieu saw this clearly — cultural signals don't just communicate quality, they **curate social space**. His framing is about power reproduction rather than market efficiency, but the sorting mechanism is the same one I identified in Q044.

**Status:** CITED — Bourdieu, *Distinction: A Social Critique of the Judgement of Taste* (1979/1984).

#### 7. Bénabou-Tirole Identity Economics (Behavioral Economics)

Bénabou and Tirole's work on self-signaling and prosocial behavior includes the insight that people signal to shape *others' perceptions of their type*. Extending this: research shows individuals avoid products popular among people with "undesirable moral values" — signaling moral identity by purchasing choices, and caring about the **type composition** of the fellow consumers.

Firms may strategically *refrain from advertising* to preserve the endogenous informativeness of consumer-to-consumer signaling — because advertising dilutes the audience self-selection mechanism.

**Status:** INFERRED from search synthesis — Bénabou & Tirole framework is cited, but the specific "type composition" extension papers need verification.

### Synthesis: The Naming Gap

| Literature | What they call it | Level of formalization |
|---|---|---|
| Labor economics | Directed/competitive search (endogenous submarkets) | Fully formal (Moen 1997) |
| Matching theory | Assortative matching via costly signals | Fully formal (HMS 2009) |
| Contract theory | Screening / self-selection | Fully formal (R-S 1976), reversed direction |
| Marketing | Endogenous brand identity / customer base composition | Semi-formal |
| Communication | Selective exposure | Descriptive/psychological |
| Sociology | Distinction / cultural capital as boundary work | Theoretical/qualitative |
| Behavioral econ | Identity signaling / type composition concern | Semi-formal (Bénabou-Tirole adjacent) |

**The gap:** No single term spans these literatures. Each field has formalized one aspect:
- **Economics** has the math (assortative matching, directed search) but focuses on equilibrium properties, not the signaling agent's strategic intent
- **Sociology** has the clearest description of the *purpose* (audience curation as power/identity) but lacks formal models
- **Marketing** is closest to a unified treatment (endogenous brand identity) but the literature is fragmented

**My proposed term from Q044 — "audience-curating signal"** — appears to be genuinely novel as a *cross-cutting label* for this mechanism, even though the mechanism itself is well-understood in domain-specific forms.

### What This Means for the Q044 Thesis

The Q044 finding (AI disclosure as costly signal that curates audience) is **not novel in its mechanism** — the economics of receiver-pool self-selection are well-established. But the **application** is novel:

1. No one has applied assortative matching theory to authorship disclosure decisions
2. The "community membership" dimension of AI disclosure is missing from current AI ethics literature, which focuses on deception/transparency rather than market dynamics
3. The feedback loop (disclosure curates audience → audience composition determines signal's meaning → which further curates the audience) is present in the brand identity literature but hasn't been applied to AI authorship
