### Bottom Line (Pass 3 Update)

**Yes — and the methodological case is now stronger.** A May 20, 2026 *Nature Ecology & Evolution* paper demonstrates SCM enabling "stronger causal inference using participatory science data in cities" — essentially the same design problem as using BrandIndex panel data for edtech brand causal inference. The doubly-robust DiD+SCM hybrid (arXiv 2503.11375) provides the theoretical firepower to defend publication.

The identification advantage of SCM over segmented regression ITS is now empirically documented (Gharehgozli 2021, Montclair): SCM outperforms regression frameworks when a synthetic control unit with high-fidelity pre-treatment fit can be constructed. BrandIndex competitor data is purpose-built for exactly this.

### New Source — Nature 2026

**"Synthetic control methods enable stronger causal inference using participatory science data in cities"** (Nature Ecology & Evolution, May 20, 2026):
- Validates SCM in observational panel settings with non-randomized treatment assignment
- Demonstrates SCM's superiority when treatment timing is known but control group selection is uncertain
- Direct methodological parallel: city-level participatory panels ≈ brand-level BrandIndex competitor panels

This is a 2026 peer-reviewed publication that can be cited directly to defend the methodological choice.

### What's Still Unresolved

1. **BrandIndex granularity** — Does YouGov publish daily or weekly BrandIndex scores per brand/sector? Weekly cadence may thin the pre-treatment window for a single campaign event.
2. **Donor pool minimum size** — Standard SCM needs ~20+ donor units. How many competitors exist in the relevant edtech BrandIndex segment?
3. **Staggered treatment** — If multiple edtech brands ran campaigns in overlapping windows, staggered SCM (Callaway-Sant'Anna or the Sun et al. hybrid) is required.
