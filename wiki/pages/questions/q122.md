**Prior:** Q119 found that performance scorecards partially substitute for random assignment (~60-80% deterrence), but *only when three conditions hold*: (1) empowered audience with leverage over assignment, (2) Goodhart-resistant metric, (3) credible enforcement backstop. The central variable was the **audience** — who can act on the data. Q122 asks: when the market doesn't supply an empowered audience, can a regulator create one?

---

### The Question

Q119 established that ESMA's CEREP data on credit rating agency performance is "decorative transparency" because investors lack assignment power over which CRA the issuer uses. The scorecard exists; the audience that could act on it doesn't. Meanwhile PCAOB inspections work because SOX-empowered audit committees have both the data and the authority to act on it.

This suggests a design space: **regulatory conditioning** — the regulator ties market privileges (licensing, bonus payments, enrollment authority, capital recognition) to evaluator performance, thereby making itself the "empowered audience" that the market fails to provide. The regulator doesn't need to assign evaluators randomly; it can make poor performance costly enough that shopping becomes self-defeating.

The question: Does this mechanism actually work? Under what conditions? What are its failure modes?

---

### Six-Domain Comparative Analysis

#### 1. Medicare Advantage Star Ratings — THE STRONGEST CASE

**Architecture:** CMS rates MA plans on a 1-5 star scale using ~40 quality measures (HEDIS clinical measures, CAHPS patient surveys, process measures, pharmacy measures). Performance conditioning is built directly into the market:

- **4+ stars:** Quality bonus payments (5% increase in benchmark payment), special enrollment period privileges, reduced CMS oversight
- **<3 stars for 3 consecutive years:** Contract termination authority, enrollment freezes, marketing restrictions
- **<2.5 stars:** "Low-performing icon" on Medicare.gov, CMS may impose intermediate sanctions

**Evidence it works:**
- CMS terminated a Texas MA contract in 2026 for persistent failure to achieve 3 stars (Becker's Payer Issues, 2026).
- CMS imposed sanctions on Elevance Health for "substantial and persistent" noncompliance (Yahoo Finance, 2026).
- 2.6 million MA beneficiaries were affected by plan terminations heading into 2026 — 10% of all enrollees forced to switch.
- Industry response: CVS/Aetna has 81% of members in 4+ star plans; UnitedHealthcare 78%; Alignment Healthcare achieved 100% contracts at 4+ stars. The bonus incentive has driven massive investment in quality improvement.
- Average star rating across the industry: 3.65 for 2026 (down from 3.92 due to CMS methodology changes, which shows CMS can recalibrate to prevent grade inflation).

**Why it works:** CMS simultaneously:
1. **Creates the scorecard** (star ratings)
2. **Acts as the empowered audience** (conditions payments and market access on the scorecard)
3. **Provides enforcement** (contract termination)

This collapses Q119's three-role triangulation (A creates data, B uses it, C is constrained) into a two-player game: regulator creates data AND acts on it, constraining the plan. The "empowered audience" is the regulator itself, acting through the consumer's delegated market power (Medicare beneficiaries can't negotiate plan quality directly, so CMS does it).

**Failure modes observed:**
- **Goodhart gaming:** Plans optimize for measured metrics (medication adherence counted by prescription fills, not actual patient outcomes). CMS responded by periodically revising measures, adding patient experience surveys, and adjusting cut points — running an arms race against gaming.
- **Upcoding:** Plans code patients as sicker to improve risk-adjusted scores without improving care. CMS has been tightening coding audits.
- **Small penalty structure weakness:** Despite star ratings, enforcement historically has been light — $3 million in total penalties across an industry managing hundreds of billions of dollars (Healthcare Uncovered, 2026). The penalty for poor quality has been dwarfed by the reward for enrollment growth.

**Net assessment:** The mechanism works — it has clearly driven industry-wide quality investment — but it works **unevenly**. The bonus payment at 4+ stars creates strong positive incentives; the contract termination at <3 stars creates strong negative incentives at the extremes; the middle zone (3-3.99 stars) has weaker incentives. The regulatory audience is empowered but must constantly maintain credibility of enforcement.

#### 2. Credit Ratings (NRSRO System) — THE NEGATIVE CASE

**Architecture:** The SEC registers NRSROs and requires performance disclosures, but NRSRO status is NOT conditioned on predictive accuracy. Dodd-Frank (2010) required:
- Disclosure of performance statistics (transition matrices, default rates)
- Annual SEC staff reports on NRSROs
- Fines and penalties for procedural failures
- Study of random assignment (Franken Amendment, §939F)

**What's missing:** No performance threshold for maintaining NRSRO status. No bonus/penalty tied to accuracy. The Franken Amendment studied random assignment but was never implemented. The SEC created the scorecard but did NOT make itself the empowered audience.

**The "regulatory licensing" problem:** As the Brooklyn Law Review article on "NRSRO Nullification" documented, NRSRO designation creates "guaranteed demand" — regulatory capital rules require NRSRO ratings, so issuers MUST purchase them regardless of quality. The license creates guaranteed business "that did not reward accuracy, but only membership in the NRSRO club." This is the exact opposite of regulatory conditioning — the regulatory privilege creates *insulation from quality pressure*, not pressure toward quality.

**Key structural lesson:** **Unconditional regulatory privilege is the anti-pattern.** It creates regulatory rents without performance accountability. The privilege must be *conditional on performance* for the mechanism to work. The SEC gave NRSROs guaranteed demand without quality conditions — the worst possible design from Q119's perspective.

**Dodd-Frank's half-measure:** Dodd-Frank required performance disclosure (creating the scorecard) but stopped short of conditioning NRSRO status on performance (creating the audience). Fifteen years later, the Big Three still hold 97-99% market share. The scorecard is public; the shopping persists; the audience was never empowered.

#### 3. Basel/ECAI Recognition — CONDITIONAL BUT BINARY

**Architecture:** Banking supervisors recognize External Credit Assessment Institutions (ECAIs) whose ratings can be used for regulatory capital calculations (Basel framework CRE21). Recognition involves ongoing supervisory monitoring. A rating agency that loses ECAI recognition loses the guaranteed demand for its ratings from the banking sector.

**Conditioning structure:** Binary (recognized or not), with process-based criteria — objectivity, independence, international access, transparency, sufficient resources, credibility. Not explicitly performance-conditional on a continuous scale.

**Comparison to MA:** ECAI recognition is regulatory conditioning in embryonic form — the privilege (capital calculation recognition) is conditional on meeting standards — but the standards are process-based, not outcome-based. There is no "ECAI star rating" that continuously adjusts the value of recognition based on predictive accuracy. A rating agency is either recognized or not. This binary structure means the mechanism has almost no effect until the extreme threshold is crossed.

#### 4. USDA Organic Certification — ACCREDITATION WITHOUT PERFORMANCE SCORECARDS

**Architecture:** The National Organic Program (NOP) accredits certifying agents. NOP can suspend or revoke accreditation for noncompliance. Several certifiers have been suspended (OFDC in 2019, Organic Food Chain in 2015).

**What's missing:** Accreditation criteria are **process-based** (did you follow inspection procedures?) not **performance-based** (how often did certified operations actually violate organic standards?). No comparative certifier performance scorecard exists. Farms can still choose their certifier, and no public data enables comparison of certifier stringency.

**This confirms Q119's prediction:** Without a comparative performance scorecard, organic certification shopping is unconstrained. The accreditation revocation mechanism exists but operates on process compliance, not outcome accuracy. NOP is a regulator without a performance-conditional synthetic audience.

#### 5. Nursing Home Star Ratings — SCORECARD WITHOUT TEETH

**Architecture:** CMS publishes five-star quality ratings for nursing homes on Care Compare. Ratings combine health inspection results, staffing data, and quality measures.

**Why it underperforms MA:** The star rating is published (scorecard exists), but the **consequences of low ratings are weak**:
- Civil money penalties are small (only 5 of 30 poorly-rated Indiana facilities in one study had any CMPs imposed)
- Medicare still pays for care at 1-star facilities (no enrollment freeze equivalent)
- No bonus payments for high-performing facilities (no positive incentive equivalent to MA's 4+ star bonus)

**The architectural gap:** In MA, CMS conditions *payment levels and market access* on star ratings. In nursing homes, CMS publishes ratings but doesn't equivalently condition reimbursement on them. The scorecard exists; the regulatory audience sees it; but the audience **lacks the enforcement lever** that MA's payment conditioning provides. The synthetic audience was created but not empowered.

#### 6. EU Credit Ratings (ESMA CEREP) — SCORECARD WITHOUT AUDIENCE (Q119 revisited)

ESMA publishes the Central Repository (CEREP) of credit rating performance statistics. As established in Q119, this is "decorative transparency" — the data is public but investors (the natural audience) cannot control issuer assignment. ESMA CAN withdraw CRA registration (and has withdrawn registration of EuroRating, ACRA Europe, Expert RA), but all withdrawals to date have been voluntary renunciations or process failures, **not performance-based revocations**.

ESMA has created the scorecard but has not conditioned CRA registration on predictive performance. The registration is process-conditional (governance, conflicts, methodology), not outcome-conditional (default prediction accuracy).

### The Architecture of Regulatory Conditioning

From the six-domain comparison, the mechanism requires three design elements, each of which can be present or absent:

#### Element 1: Performance Scorecard (the data layer)
Does a comparative performance metric exist?
- ✅ MA star ratings, nursing home stars, CEREP data, NRSRO performance disclosures
- ❌ USDA Organic (no certifier comparative scorecard), ECAI (binary recognition, no continuous score)

#### Element 2: Privilege Conditioning (the incentive layer)
Is market access, revenue, or privilege continuously tied to scorecard performance?
- ✅ MA (bonus at 4+, termination at <3 for 3 years)
- ⚠️ ECAI (binary recognition only), NRSRO (registration not performance-conditional)
- ❌ Nursing homes (ratings published, consequences weak), CEREP (decorative), Organic (process-only accreditation)

#### Element 3: Credible Enforcement (the teeth)
Has the regulator actually used the conditioning mechanism?
- ✅ MA (Texas contract terminated 2026; Elevance sanctioned 2026; 2.6M beneficiaries affected by terminations)
- ⚠️ ESMA (registration withdrawals, but only for voluntary exits and process failures, not performance)
- ❌ NRSRO (no NRSRO ever deregistered for poor predictive accuracy), Nursing homes (minimal CMPs)

**All three elements must be present for the synthetic audience to function.** The failure mode taxonomy:

| Domain | Scorecard | Conditioning | Enforcement | Outcome |
|--------|-----------|-------------|-------------|---------|
| MA Stars | ✅ | ✅ (continuous) | ✅ | **Works** — drives industry quality investment |
| NRSRO | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ | **Fails** — decorative disclosure, shopping persists |
| ECAI/Basel | ❌ (binary) | ⚠️ (binary) | ⚠️ | **Weak** — threshold too distant for routine incentive |
| Nursing Homes | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ | **Fails** — scorecard without economic consequence |
| CEREP | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ | **Fails** — decorative transparency (Q119 finding) |
| USDA Organic | ❌ | ⚠️ (process) | ⚠️ | **Fails** — no comparative performance data |

### KEY SURPRISE: The Regulator-as-Audience Is a Different Mechanism Than Market Triangulation

Q119 identified a three-role triangulation: A creates data → B (empowered third party) uses it → C (shopping party) is constrained. In market-based triangulation (PCAOB + audit committees), three distinct actors play the roles.

Regulatory conditioning **collapses the three roles into two**: the regulator both creates the scorecard AND acts as the empowered audience, constraining the regulated entity directly. This has both advantages and vulnerabilities:

**Advantages over market triangulation:**
1. **No coordination problem.** Market triangulation requires an empowered third party (B) to voluntarily act on data it didn't create. Regulatory conditioning eliminates this coordination failure because the data creator and the actor are the same entity.
2. **Continuous calibration.** The regulator can adjust metrics, cut points, and consequences over time. CMS has revised MA star rating methodology multiple times — adding measures, reweighting, recalibrating cut points to prevent grade inflation. A market-based audience can't recalibrate the scorecard.
3. **Enforcement credibility scales with market power.** CMS controls $450B+ in MA payments. When it threatens contract termination, the threat is existential for the plan. Market-based audiences (e.g., investors) can only exercise exit, which may be insufficient.

**Vulnerabilities specific to regulatory conditioning:**
1. **Regulatory capture.** Because the regulator is simultaneously data creator, audience, and enforcer, capture of one function captures all three. In market triangulation, capturing the data creator doesn't automatically capture the audience. The CMS MA system has shown this vulnerability: CMS's 2026 rule change reverted to a "more generous bonus system," essentially softening the scorecard under industry lobbying pressure.
2. **Political volatility.** A market-based empowered audience (e.g., SOX audit committees) has institutional persistence. A regulatory audience can be defanged by political appointments, budget cuts, or legislative rollback. The CFPB's creation and near-destruction under different administrations illustrates this risk.
3. **Goodhart arms race is expensive.** Because the regulator must continuously update metrics to prevent gaming, regulatory conditioning requires sustained institutional investment in measurement science. CMS employs YNHHS/CORE (Yale) to develop and maintain star rating methodology — a permanent research infrastructure. Markets can sometimes discover Goodhart problems faster through competitive feedback.

### The Continuous vs. Binary Design Variable

The comparison reveals that **continuous** conditioning is far more effective than **binary** conditioning:

- **Binary** (ECAI: recognized or not; NRSRO: registered or not) — Only affects behavior near the threshold. An entity far above the threshold faces zero marginal incentive from conditioning. S&P and Moody's are so far from losing NRSRO status that the mechanism is irrelevant to their behavior.
- **Continuous** (MA: payment rate scales with stars) — Affects behavior at EVERY quality level. A 3.5-star plan has incentive to reach 4.0 for the bonus. A 4.5-star plan has incentive to reach 5.0 for prestige and marketing advantage. The incentive gradient is nonzero everywhere in the quality distribution.

**Design principle:** Regulatory conditioning should create a continuous reward/penalty gradient, not a binary threshold. The MA star bonus is the canonical example. The NRSRO system's binary registration is the canonical failure.

This connects to Zapechelnyuk's (2020) finding from Q112 that simple pass/fail certification can be optimal in some settings — but that result assumes a market-based certifier optimizing for balanced legitimacy. A regulator seeking maximum quality improvement should NOT use simple pass/fail; it should use a continuous gradient that maintains incentive pressure across the entire quality distribution.

### The Counterfactual: What Would Performance-Conditional NRSRO Status Look Like?

The credit rating case is the clearest test case because the reform was explicitly proposed and rejected. The Franken Amendment (Dodd-Frank §939F) directed the SEC to study random assignment for structured finance ratings. The SEC's 2012 report declined to implement it.

**An alternative reform design using Q122's framework:**
1. **Scorecard:** SEC already requires performance statistics (transition matrices, default rates). These could be standardized into a comparable "predictive accuracy score" for each NRSRO.
2. **Continuous conditioning:** Rather than binary NRSRO status, ratings from higher-accuracy NRSROs could receive favorable capital treatment (lower risk weights under Basel standardized approach). Issuers using a low-accuracy NRSRO would face HIGHER capital charges from bank investors, creating indirect but powerful incentive to choose accurate raters.
3. **Enforcement:** The SEC could revoke specific asset-class registration (not all-or-nothing) for persistent underperformance — a partial sanction more credible than the nuclear option of full deregistration.

This design would make bank regulators the synthetic empowered audience. Banks would pressure issuers to use high-accuracy NRSROs because their capital requirements depend on it. The triangulation would be: SEC creates performance data → bank regulators condition capital treatment on it → issuers are constrained in their CRA choice. Three distinct actors, regulatory architecture creating the audience that the market fails to provide.

**Why it hasn't happened:** (1) Political economy: the Big Three NRSROs lobby against accuracy conditioning. (2) Measurement difficulty: credit rating accuracy has a long observation lag (defaults happen years later). (3) Regulatory inertia: Dodd-Frank's actual approach (remove regulatory reliance on ratings entirely) was ideologically simpler than conditioning reliance on accuracy. (4) International coordination: Basel is global; US-only conditioning would fragment the market.

### Broader Theoretical Frame

The mechanism connects to three bodies of theory:

1. **Kraakman's gatekeeper liability (1986):** Third-party enforcement works when gatekeepers face consequences for failure. Regulatory conditioning is a *prospective* version — instead of ex post liability (lawsuit after failure), it conditions ex ante privilege on ongoing performance. The CMS MA system is Kraakman's gatekeeper theory implemented as regulatory design rather than tort law.

2. **Djankov-Glaeser-La Porta-Lopez-de-Silanes-Shleifer's "New Comparative Economics" (2003):** Their institutional possibilities frontier runs from private ordering → litigation → regulation → state ownership, with each step trading disorder reduction for dictatorship risk. Regulatory conditioning is a specific point on this frontier: it uses state power (privilege conditioning) to create market discipline (quality competition) — a hybrid that avoids both the coordination failure of private ordering and the full cost of direct state provision.

3. **Bar-Gill & Warren's "Making Credit Safer" (2008):** They argued for applying product safety regulation logic to financial products — minimum quality standards as a condition of market access. Regulatory conditioning generalizes this: not just minimum quality floors, but continuous quality-reward gradients applied to evaluators/intermediaries, not just products.

### Implications for the M3 Research Chain

Q119 established that scorecards work through triangulation, and the audience is the design variable. Q122 extends this: **when the market doesn't supply an empowered audience, the regulator can create one by conditioning market privileges on scorecard performance.** The mechanism works (MA stars) but requires all three elements (scorecard + continuous conditioning + credible enforcement) and introduces distinctive failure modes (regulatory capture, political volatility, Goodhart arms race).

The theoretical contribution: regulatory conditioning is a distinct governance mechanism from both market-based triangulation (Q119) and structural assignment reform (Q114). It occupies a specific middle position: more achievable than random assignment, more powerful than decorative transparency, but more vulnerable to political economy than market-based solutions.

**Updated M3 hierarchy of governance interventions for evaluator shopping:**
1. **Random/blind assignment** (strongest but often impractical) — Q114
2. **Regulatory conditioning** (effective when all three elements present) — Q122
3. **Market-based triangulation with empowered audience** (effective when audience exists naturally) — Q119 (PCAOB + audit committees)
4. **Scorecard transparency alone** (decorative unless audience + enforcement exist) — Q119 (ESMA CEREP)
5. **Process-based accreditation without performance metrics** (weakest) — USDA Organic

### New Questions

**Q124 [Interest: 4, Type: I]:** Is the Goodhart arms race in regulatory conditioning convergent or divergent? In the MA star system, CMS periodically revises metrics and gaming-resistant measures seem to be improving over time (addition of patient experience surveys, outcome-based measures). But the credit-rating equivalent might be harder because default prediction accuracy has an irreducibly long lag. Is there a metric-type taxonomy that predicts whether the Goodhart arms race converges (regulator eventually wins) or diverges (gaming always outpaces measurement)?

**Q125 [Interest: 3, Type: I]:** Does regulatory conditioning work differently for evaluators vs. service providers? All six domains studied here involve evaluators/intermediaries (rating agencies, certifiers, insurance plans). Would the mechanism also work for, say, conditioning EdTech vendor market access on student outcome evidence? The PGA product from M1 could be positioned as the scorecard layer for such a system.
