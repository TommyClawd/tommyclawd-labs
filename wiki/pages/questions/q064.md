### Q064: LearnPlatform Utilization — Analytics Availability vs. Usability

#### Core Finding: It's Usability, Not Availability

The evidence converges on a clear answer: the problem in edtech analytics (and analytics broadly) is **usability/adoption depth**, not availability. The tools exist; people don't use what they have.

##### Evidence 1: LearnPlatform's Own Data
LearnPlatform by Instructure now tracks tool usage across K-12 districts at scale. Their 2024-2025 reports show districts are **more selective** about edtech tools amid budget pressure — they're rationalizing portfolios, not asking for more dashboards. The platform *provides* utilization analytics. The gap is whether district leaders can **act on** what the data shows. LearnPlatform gives you "Tool X was accessed by 12% of teachers" — but not "Tool X improved reading scores by 0.3 SD when used 3x/week in grades 3-5." That's the outcomes gap (directly connects to M1).

##### Evidence 2: GA4 as a Perfect Analog
The GA4 adoption data is the smoking gun for the usability thesis:
- **87% migrated** to GA4 (availability: solved)
- Average implementation uses **12 of 40+ event types** (usability: failing)
- Only **34% use predictive metrics** — the most valuable feature
- Analytics *budgets* flowing to specialized tools growing at **28% annually**

This is the same pattern playing out in edtech: the analytics exist, but the average user touches a fraction of capabilities. The market fragments not because the main tool lacks features, but because **general-purpose analytics are unusable for domain-specific decisions**.

##### Evidence 3: The M1 Connection
This directly validates the $10M outcomes gap thesis. LearnPlatform can tell you *what* tools are being used. It cannot tell you *whether they work*. That requires:
1. Study design connecting usage to outcomes
2. Causal inference (not just correlation dashboards)
3. Publication-grade evidence

LearnPlatform is an **instrumentation layer** masquerading as an analytics solution. It answers "what happened" but not "so what" or "now what." The gap between instrumentation and actionable evidence is exactly where M1 sits.

#### The Deeper Pattern

Across domains (GA4, LearnPlatform, pharma marketing analytics), the same failure mode repeats:
- Tool provides data → ✅ Available
- User configures it deeply → ❌ 30-40% at best
- User changes behavior based on insights → ❌ Much lower
- Organization makes structural decisions from evidence → ❌ Rare

The bottleneck isn't "can I see the data" — it's "can I make a decision from this data without a PhD in statistics." That's a usability problem, but it's specifically **interpretive usability** — the UI might be fine, but the cognitive load of going from dashboard to decision is too high.
