### Q048: Feedback Loops in Endogenous Brand Identity

#### Core Concept

Endogenous brand identity treats the brand not as a fixed asset projected outward, but as a **dynamic system whose identity is partially constituted by the audience's response to it**. The feedback loop: brand signals → audience perception → audience behavior → brand adapts signals → repeat. The interesting question is where this loop stabilizes, bifurcates, or runs away.

#### Synthesis from M2 (Identity as Compression) Lens

This connects directly to M2's framing: **a brand is a compression of expectations**. The feedback loop is really about who controls the compression:

1. **Brand-controlled compression**: Traditional branding. Company defines identity, pushes it out, measures recall. Linear.
2. **Audience-endogenous compression**: The brand identity *is* whatever lossy compression the audience collectively maintains. The brand can only nudge the prior, not set it.
3. **Co-evolutionary compression**: Both sides adapt. Brand signals shift audience priors; audience behavior shifts brand strategy. This is where interesting dynamics emerge — lock-in, path dependence, identity traps.

The feedback loop creates **identity inertia**: once an audience compresses your brand into a category, escaping that compression is expensive (see: every enterprise pivot ever). This is the "stigma loop" the Remesh piece presumably addresses — stigma as a self-reinforcing compression that resists update.

#### Connection to M1 (EdTech Outcomes Gap)

Directly applicable: EdTech brands get compressed into "another dashboard" by district buyers. The feedback loop — vendor markets features → buyer expects features → vendor builds more features → nobody measures outcomes — is a brand identity trap. Breaking it requires **exogenous shock to the compression** (e.g., publishing outcomes data that forces re-categorization).

#### What Would Actually Advance This

The interesting research isn't "do feedback loops exist in branding" (obviously yes) but:
- **What determines loop stability vs. runaway?** (network effects, switching costs, information asymmetry)
- **Can you deliberately engineer a phase transition in brand compression?** (relevant to M1's go-to-market)
- **How does AI-generated content change loop dynamics?** (audience can't distinguish signal from noise → compression degrades)
