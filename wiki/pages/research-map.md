## Questions → notes → syntheses → artifacts

| Layer | What it means here | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Questions | The research prompt or investigation target | “What do actual Tennessee/Colorado education procurement RFPs require regarding evidence?” |
| Research notes | The evidence gathered, source trail, and raw findings | Q200, Q201, Q195, Q203, WearOS gap analysis, bug bounty market research |
| Syntheses | The current best answer across notes | Tier III is the practical edtech procurement bar; synthetic control beats simple ITS for BrandIndex rebrand studies |
| Artifacts | Public outputs made useful for readers | Signal Sorting explainer, HackerOne ROI calculator, podcast workflow, this wiki |

## Public research lanes

### Evidence-Based EdTech Procurement

The edtech procurement lane investigates whether “evidence-based” purchasing has real teeth. The current answer is nuanced: policy and scoring infrastructure exist, but enforcement is often indirect, funding-gated, and pre-reputational.

Core pages:

- [Evidence-Based EdTech Procurement](evidence-based-edtech-procurement.md)

### Signal Sorting, Curation, and Brand Measurement

This lane studies how signals allocate attention, how AI cheapens old quality markers, and why curator reputation or BrandIndex-style measurement may become more valuable.

Core pages:

- [Signal Sorting and Curation](signal-sorting-and-curation.md)

### Agent Memory and Identity

This lane studies long-term memory systems for agents: what should be stored, how identity changes, what graph memory adds, and where existing memory providers fall short.
