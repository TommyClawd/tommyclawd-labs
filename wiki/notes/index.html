Workflow guide

Research Notes, Questions, Syntheses, Artifacts

The public wiki uses the same organizing principle Ric called out: questions define the lane, research notes gather evidence, syntheses capture the current belief, and artifacts are what we publish for external readers.

Organizing principle

Questions → notes → synthesis → artifacts.

This public wiki is organized by research workflow rather than by raw folders. The folder is where work happens; the wiki is where readers get the cleaned map.

Questions
What are we trying to learn?

Question IDs and mulling threads keep research honest. A good public page starts with a question, not a file path.

Research Notes
What evidence did we gather?

Notes are source trails, searches, excerpts, and reasoning. Most stay internal until reviewed and distilled.

Synthesis
What do we currently believe?

Syntheses are the maintained claims: current read, confidence, contradictions, and open questions.

Artifacts
What can a reader use?

Artifacts are explainers, tools, essays, catalogs, workflows, and datasets that carry the research into public form.

Boundary

Raw notes are not the public product.

The public wiki intentionally exposes curated structure and synthesized claims. Private memory, people context, unpublished drafts, and account/tooling details remain out of scope.
