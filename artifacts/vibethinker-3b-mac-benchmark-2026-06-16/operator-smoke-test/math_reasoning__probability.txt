Thus answer: \boxed{\frac{1}{4}}.</think>To find the probability of getting exactly 3 heads in 4 flips of a fair coin, we use the binomial distribution.

1. **Number of possible outcomes:**  
   Each flip has 2 possibilities (H or T). For 4 flips, there are \(2^4 = 16\) equally likely sequences.

2. **Number of favorable outcomes (exactly 3 heads):**  
   We need to choose which 3 of the 4 flips are heads. The number of ways to do this is given by the binomial coefficient:
   \[
   \binom{4}{3} = \frac{4!}{3!\,1!} = 4.
   \]
   So there are 4 sequences with exactly 3 heads (HHHT, HHTH, HTHH, THHH).

3. **Probability:**  
   \[
   P(\text{exactly 3 heads}) = \frac{\text{favorable outcomes}}{\text{total outcomes}} = \frac{4}{16} = \frac{1}{4}.
   \]
