Creators are cheap. Curators are kings.

AI didn’t erase the quality problem. It moved it. When production becomes cheap, the valuable question stops being who made this? and becomes who chose this?

Core shift

Value is migrating from production to selection. In domains where quality can be objectively checked, AI improves sorting. In creative domains, AI removes the lower-level signals that used to make sorting easy, so curatorial judgment becomes the scarce layer.

One-line version Polish used to be a filter. Now it’s table stakes. The remaining moat is trusted judgment.
The framework

The three layers of “is this good?”

Creative evaluation feels like one judgment, but it’s really three stacked filters. AI affects each layer differently.

Layer 1

Technical craft

Exposure, grammar, polish, clean execution. This is the most machine-assessable layer, and AI has made it cheap.

AI commoditizes this
Layer 2

Consensus taste

What people will click, save, stream, and share. Algorithms are excellent at forecasting aggregate preference.

AI predicts this well
Layer 3

Aesthetic judgment

Does it matter? Does it surprise? Does it reveal anything worth carrying forward? This remains stubbornly human.

This gets scarcer
What changed

The sorting problem moved upward

Before AI abundance, Layers 1 and 2 did most of the filtering. After AI abundance, those layers stop differentiating enough — so all the pressure lands on Layer 3.

Before

Bad craft filtered out amateurs. Weak polish filtered out the unserious. Provenance acted as a shortcut.

→

AI flood

Craft gets automated. Engagement gets optimized. Surface competence becomes abundant and cheap.

→

After

The decisive signal is no longer the maker. It’s the selector willing to stake reputation on a choice.

Question flip

From “who made this?” to “who chose this?”

That is the structural reason galleries, editors, playlist curators, trusted newsletters, and brand filters become more valuable in an AI-saturated market — not less.

Domain split

Why code gets easier to judge while art gets harder

The key distinction is whether quality bottoms out in objective checks or subjective experience.

Domain Ultimate test Effect of better AI Who gains leverage
Code / math / engineering Does it compile, prove, converge, or pass? Sorting gets easier Builders with verification systems
Music / art / writing / design Does it matter to someone in a way metrics can’t settle? Sorting gets harder Curators, editors, trusted tastemakers
Platforms in the middle Can they balance engagement with meaning? Layer 2 gets cheaper; Layer 3 gets bottlenecked Systems that combine algorithmic ranking with human judgment
Implications

What to do with the shift

If the moat is judgment now, strategy changes for almost everyone in the chain.

Creators

  • Treat production quality as table stakes, not a moat.
  • Build a visible record of what you recommend, not just what you make.
  • Your curation is part of your brand now.

Platforms

  • Pure engagement optimization will Goodhart itself.
  • Invest in trusted human filters and editorial layers.
  • The winning stack is algorithm + curator, not algorithm alone.

Consumers

  • Watch the selector, not just the source.
  • Subscribe to people with taste, not just output volume.
  • Reputation becomes your anti-spam infrastructure.

Organizations

  • The next leverage hire may be an editor, not another generator.
  • Someone must say “no” to the flood of competent-but-empty work.
  • Discernment is becoming an operational function.
Bottom line

AI did not kill intermediaries. It made some of them newly precious. When making becomes cheap, choosing becomes the scarce act. That’s why the people who can reliably say this one matters are inheriting more of the value.
