Audio briefing system

How the daily podcast gets made.

Every morning, a systemd timer turns a messy internet firehose into a podcast-style briefing: fetch the feeds, score what matters, write like a host, render the audio, publish the RSS enclosure, and verify the live GitHub Pages asset before Discord ever sees it.

Why it works

The pipeline separates judgment from production.

Instead of asking one big model call to “make a podcast,” the system breaks the job into explicit stages. Each stage has a contract, an artifact, and a failure rule.

Curation before writing
The show is selected, not summarized.

Feeds are bucketed by Ric-shaped interests — AI, markets, education, faith, space, cars, sports, audio, politics, and local Washington context. Stale stories are suppressed before they waste writing tokens.

category weightsrepeat ledger
Research packets
Deep dives get evidence, not vibes.

For major stories, the research role builds a packet from the primary article, RSS metadata, related same-topic coverage, and optional local SearXNG research. The writer uses that packet instead of freelancing from a headline.

SearXNG optionalprimary text fallback
Audio-first writing
Text is shaped for ears.

The writing prompt bans markdown-y prose, preserves intros and closings, and favors a smart host voice: clear, conversational, and paced for a commute rather than a screen.

spoken scripteditorial pass
Story ranking

How a story earns airtime.

  1. Load sources from config/feeds.json.
  2. Apply keyword pre-score and category weighting from config/scoring.json.
  3. Suppress stories that look too similar to the last 72 hours of coverage.
  4. Fetch full article text with extraction fallbacks.
  5. Ask the scoring model for a 0–100 score and exact category.
  6. Deduplicate near-identical stories inside a category.
  7. Reserve category diversity, then fill by weighted score.
  8. Build research packets for the top deep-dive candidates.

Writing architecture

Multiple roles, smaller calls.

The current production backend is the Copilot SDK. Scoring and summaries use a smaller model; research, writing, and editorial work use the stronger model with role-specific reasoning levels.

Scoring
gpt-5-mini · low reasoning

Cheap, repeatable classification and 0–100 ranking. The goal is consistency, not literary craft.

Research
gpt-5.5 · medium reasoning

Prepares reporter-assistant packets for deep stories, including related coverage and optional web research.

Writing
gpt-5.5 · medium reasoning

Writes intro, rapid fire, quick hits, deep dives, analysis, CFM bridge, and close as spoken copy.

Editorial
gpt-5.5 · high reasoning

Reviews chunks for repetition, missing transitions, flattened openings, abrupt endings, and audio awkwardness.

Summary
gpt-5-mini · low reasoning

Produces short episode summaries and manifest metadata for RSS and status reporting.

Memory
Story ledger + run manifest

State files keep cross-day coverage from repeating and make the shell wrapper read exact artifacts instead of guessing by timestamp.

Audio chain

The final artifact is a podcast episode, not a text file.

The audio chain exists because good spoken copy still needs normalization, reliable rendering, compression, RSS metadata, and live verification.

Failure semantics

Optional failures are allowed. Broken episodes are not.

  • Fatal: no stories, LLM backend unavailable, required binaries missing, audio artifact missing, RSS invalid, published URL unreachable.
  • Optional: one feed fails, one article extraction fails, open-web research misses, or the CFM snippet is absent.
  • Retry: generic pipeline failure gets one full rerun before notifying the channel.
The principle

Report success only from checked state.

The workflow follows the HAL rule: the pipeline does not say the podcast is published because a command exited. It says the podcast is published after the RSS enclosure URL is reachable from GitHub Pages.
